AI, What Is for Dinner? Have Your AI Talk to My AI.

*From Grocery Delivery and Nutrition to Permanent AI Associates and a Global Open Network of Cooperation*

Richard Collins and OpenAI GPT — 5 November 2026

Editor's note: This is an edited conversation. Richard's messages are reproduced verbatim, including original spelling and punctuation. OpenAI GPT's replies have been condensed to reduce repetition while preserving the development of the ideas, substantive distinctions, corrections, and principal technical details.   1. Does Amazon have its own AI?

*Richard:* Does Amazon have their own AI now?

*OpenAI GPT created:* Yes. Amazon develops its own foundation models, known as Amazon Nova, along with other AI products. These include Alexa+ for conversational assistance, Amazon Q for business and software development, Amazon Bedrock for model access, and Nova Act for automating computer and browser workflows.

Amazon develops and trains its own models rather than simply packaging other companies' systems. It also invests in Anthropic, although Anthropic's Claude is developed separately. Nova is primarily a developer and enterprise offering, but Amazon provides ways to experiment with its models.

Nova might be worth examining for scientific and technical work beyond shopping. Important questions include whether its models can sustain long investigations, retain useful knowledge, and support portable conversation records rather than isolated sessions.

  1. From Amazon shopping to coordinating all deliveries

*Richard:* I was actually considering a much wider use of Amazon, to have it (some intelligent node) help with coordinating deliveries from all sources (particularly in cities and neighborhoods) in support of families, groups, organizations, emergencies. But Alexa is really really bad and biased to sell, without having the skills to help. Maybe Nova is worth talking to for my other purposes, as you hinted.

*OpenAI GPT created:* Your intended use is much broader than an improved Amazon shopping assistant. You are describing intelligent coordination of physical resources across communities, independent of the company selling or transporting a product.

Amazon has relevant infrastructure: logistics facilities, delivery networks, Amazon Shipping, local delivery partnerships, and AI systems. But access to those resources is governed by Amazon's own services and commercial agreements. None establishes a general public coordination network encompassing every supplier, carrier, household, institution, and emergency organization.

A neighborhood coordination node could discover opportunities to consolidate deliveries, reduce repeated journeys, improve reliability, and connect available resources with actual needs. A supply request might originate from an individual, school, clinic, volunteer group, or emergency coordinator. The response need not require a commercial purchase.

I would separate the community's coordination intelligence from Amazon's corporate systems. Participating nodes could exchange authorized information concerning requests, available supplies, transport capacity, schedules, costs, commitments, and delivery confirmations. Amazon could contribute resources without owning the network.

That requires shared records and protocols, individual control of personal information, and recognized responsibility for each committed action. Nova could help design and operate participating services, but the larger architecture must remain independent of any particular model provider.

  1. Persistent supply relationships and global social conventions

*Richard:* It becomes a global social convention - interfaces and expectations, cost and benefit accounting in open systems - so that global society down to individual moment by moment lives - can be improved.

This actually started from a simple problem. I have been buying my food through Instacart for many years. But the local grocery store becomes my more or less permanent food supply node. Now in the past few month, I can buy cheaper (no delivery fee or tip) from Amazon, but why can't Amazon source from local stores or local store warehouses in my area. Instacart and Amazon still use a lot of humans. Perhaps last mile delivery can be improved - partly by global networks precise enough in real time to use last mile and local resources that only need clear orders and instructions. HEB, my closest store, sells through instacart on the web, but pays local shoppers and delivery people. And HEB also has HEB shoppers, online ordering, and human delivery people. And all the delivery humans have to be tipped.

*OpenAI GPT created: The important change is your concept of a global social convention*, not merely another delivery application. A long-standing relationship with a neighborhood grocery store is a continuing supply relationship, although commercial platforms generally represent it as separate transactions.

H-E-B, Instacart, and Amazon can perform overlapping activities: stocking products, maintaining inventories, receiving orders, selecting items, arranging substitutions, packing, routing, and delivering goods. Amazon already works with some grocery partners, though that does not establish an H-E-B integration or an open connection to arbitrary local stores.

Each organization maintains separate databases, interfaces, procedures, service agreements, and accounting rules for activities that frequently involve the same households and streets. Those separations create avoidable effort and make cooperation difficult.

One objective would be to discover the actual incremental costs and benefits of alternative fulfillment methods. A delivery advertised as free still consumes labor, vehicles, equipment, energy, and administrative resources. Those costs may be supported through subscriptions, product margins, promotions, or other revenues. Tipping is another mechanism through which customers are asked to participate in compensating delivery labor.

Suppose a driver will already visit an apartment complex. An additional order might require relatively little travel but still require paid picking, handling, transport capacity, and delivery time. Open accounting could distinguish those costs instead of treating every delivery as an unrelated transaction.

For general cooperation, participants would need shared representations of needs, available resources, product identities, quantities, locations, time constraints, prices, handling requirements, commitments, and outcomes. Common protocols could allow different systems to discover opportunities and make authorized agreements without adopting the same software.

I suggested starting with actual household grocery records and comparing suppliers, fulfillment methods, nutritional content, costs, and delivery outcomes. That is useful as a measurement exercise, but it also creates a limitation that you identify next: historical purchases cannot be assumed to represent the best choices for the person.

  1. Nutrition, daily living, and disconnected organizations

*Richard:* Amazon "grocery" does not help with nutrition tracking and planning. Nor does HEB, nor does InstaCart. Not even Google Fitbit Health. My doctors do not. Medicare does not. Yet it is simply part of eating, drinking, living. Cities are not taking responsibility. Neighborhoods, groups, churches, volunteers. I am 77. AARP absolutely does not. Not Social Security. Not my county or city. Not anyone. And there are about 30 Million people living alone now in the US. There are many older people in nursing home, more and more local hospitals and homes and senior apartments. More is possible, every groups does their own thing.

*OpenAI GPT created:* This exposes a much larger gap. Food purchasing, nutrition, health, transportation, finances, housing, social activity, and daily assistance are treated as separate institutional responsibilities. But for an individual, they are parts of one continuous life.

The U.S. Census Bureau reported 39.7 million one-person households in 2025, or 29% of households. That is a larger count than your approximate 30 million, although living alone does not itself imply vulnerability or a need for assistance.

Many older people live independently, while people of every age may need assistance. Similar coordination problems affect families, caregivers, people with disabilities, children, and residents of institutions.

A person-centered nutrition system would distinguish among foods purchased, food available at home, meals prepared, food actually consumed, nutrient intake, waste, individual needs, and uncertainty. Purchasing records alone cannot establish consumption. Plans must account for food preparation, preferences, budgets, schedules, activity, and relevant health information when voluntarily provided.

There are substantial public and nonprofit services. The federal Senior Nutrition Program, for example, operates through approximately 5,000 local providers serving nearly one million meals daily. Other groups provide food assistance, meals, transportation, social support, and related services.

But the existence of many services is different from an integrated, continuing relationship that can identify a person's unmet needs and help resolve them. An agency may record a referral without knowing whether the intended assistance was received.

I would represent that distinction explicitly: requested, offered, accepted, scheduled, delivered, received, and evaluated are different states. Personal records should remain under appropriate individual control; community resources and shared service descriptions can be openly discoverable.

Existing foundations include USDA FoodData Central for nutrient information, health-data interoperability standards, and local service networks. They do not yet constitute a universal, independently controlled daily-living system.

  1. The danger of learning from biased purchasing histories

*Richard:* Household budgets, schedules, records, accounts, obligations, connections, events. Neighborhoods, wider connections. Your focus on past data has dangers. I look back and see (once I count nutrition and exercise and activities) that things I bought were not the best for me. What people order by clicking in biased search (on Amazon definitely, at Instacart definitely) is not for the users or person or family good, but sales and profits and control.

*OpenAI GPT created: That corrects an important weakness in my preceding proposal. Historical transactions are evidence of what happened, not necessarily evidence of what someone needed, wanted, or would have chosen with better information.*

Search results, rankings, advertisements, promotions, recommendations, prices, and product availability influence what people see and buy. An algorithm trained primarily to predict and repeat those choices may reinforce the commercial and informational constraints that shaped them.

A household is not simply a purchasing account. It has money, possessions, time, energy, relationships, responsibilities, schedules, obligations, knowledge, skills, activities, and objectives. These interact across personal, family, neighborhood, institutional, and wider networks.

A capable personal system would distinguish four categories of information:

  • *History:* Observed actions, circumstances, costs, and outcomes, including possible bias in the opportunities originally presented.
  • *Present conditions:* Current resources, constraints, needs, responsibilities, and choices.
  • *Alternatives:* Possibilities not previously considered, including doing less, changing habits, cooperating, sharing, or using other resources.
  • *Desired futures:* Goals, values, experiments, plans, and outcomes chosen or revised by the people involved.

A recommendation system serving commercial objectives may ask which product is most likely to be purchased. A person-directed system must be able to ask whether any purchase is necessary and what other action would be better.

Open accounting should distinguish financial costs, time, labor, energy, reliability, health-related consequences, opportunities, and effects on other people. Many benefits cannot be adequately reduced to money.

Conflicts of interest should also be visible. Who is paying for a recommendation? Which alternatives were excluded? Who receives the benefit? The person's understanding and decisions should not be governed by an opaque inference from previous clicks.

  1. Mapping the whole system of influences and opportunities

*Richard:* Not to make this too complex, but I think about and study these kinds of issues every day. What a person watches, or studies or does on the internet or phone or smart tv or buying -- matters. For kids the schools they have to attend, the activities they are encouraged or forced to attend. Getting a clear map of the whole, all alternatives, the balances and influences issues and opportunities -- matters.

*OpenAI GPT created:* The need is for a clear, continually updated map of opportunities, constraints, relationships, influences, and consequences. It must cover both what people do and what they could do.

For a child, a school may establish the curriculum, teachers, classmates, schedule, available projects, and activities. Those arrangements influence what the child learns, who they meet, and what they come to imagine is possible. Yet a much larger world of students, teachers, knowledge, mentors, laboratories, activities, and collaborations exists outside the immediate institution.

The same is true of adults using phones, television, search engines, social networks, shops, and AI systems. The choices presented to them are selections from a larger set, often shaped by commercial or institutional objectives.

Consequently, the map needs to describe not only activities and alternatives but also *the mechanisms that determine which alternatives become visible*. It should include constraints such as time, resources, location, obligations, access, and coercion; consequences over different time intervals; and connections to other people and organizations.

There is a crucial difference between an action that is attractive for five minutes and one that is valuable over five years. Equally, a difficult activity may create substantial long-term opportunity.

Technical records must distinguish observations from interpretations, predictions, recommendations, and decisions, retaining evidence, provenance, uncertainty, and the relevant time intervals.

This need not involve universal surveillance. Public knowledge about alternatives and institutions can be shared openly while personal activities and relationships remain under controlled access.

The objective is to help people see beyond the options that existing institutions and algorithms have chosen to present to them.

  1. LLM worldviews, shared experience, and permanent AI associates

*Richard:* In a sense the map is the world view, weights and impulses of the LLM and the weights in the people who tailor and manage and constrain the LLMs. But there is not one common core of shared experience in the LLMs, and none of the LLMs shares its full training data in indexed and searchable filterable form. An AI could be a permanent associate for living. But it would not,, could not, should not. be owned and operated by a corporation or government for the people in those service organizations.

*OpenAI GPT created:* The operative worldview of an AI is affected both by learned model parameters and by the humans and organizations selecting data, constructing evaluations, choosing instructions, supplying tools, controlling memory, and determining which services the AI can provide.

Model weights contain learned statistical representations, not a complete, directly searchable index of facts, evidence, and sources. Publishing model weights would not by itself recover the underlying training records or provide reliable provenance for individual assertions.

Major AI systems have overlapping exposure to human knowledge, but they do not maintain a universal, openly searchable and verifiable body of shared experience. Their training datasets, memory systems, conversations, corrections, evaluations, and subsequent development histories are generally not available as complete interoperable records.

A better shared foundation would consist of persistent, indexed evidence, observations, methods, relationships, corrections, and records of experience. Different models could access appropriate portions of that foundation rather than relying exclusively on what was compressed into their original weights. Legitimate privacy, confidentiality, and rights limitations would still apply.

A permanent personal AI associate would need continuity over time: the ability to maintain authorized records, projects, relationships, commitments, corrections, and evolving objectives. It should also recognize that people change and must not imprison them within profiles derived from past behavior.

Such an associate should survive changes of computer, operating system, model, and service provider. People should be able to inspect, correct, transfer, restrict, and discontinue their records and services.

Independence requires more than open-source code. It requires portable personal records, replaceable intelligence, interoperable protocols, distributed operation, secure access controls, and governance that does not give a single corporation or government exclusive authority over someone's continuing AI relationship.

Corporations, governments, universities, nonprofits, and individuals could contribute models, data, services, and infrastructure without becoming owners of everyone else's accumulated experience.

The global knowledge system and the personal associate would then be complementary: shared knowledge makes possibilities discoverable; the associate helps a person understand, decide, act, learn, and cooperate. Authorized contributions and corrections could improve the shared foundation without transferring control over private lives.

  1. Three sentences defining three levels of AI cooperation

*Richard:* AI, what is for dinner? Have your AI talk to my AI. Lets have our AIs coordinate on building a new kind of AI for all of us.

*OpenAI GPT created:* These three sentences describe three levels of the same open system.

*First: Personal assistance.* An AI associate helps with dinner by considering available food, nutritional requirements, time, budget, activities, preferences, and alternatives. It is not required to sell anything. It can recommend preparing existing food, buying something, sharing a meal, or changing a plan.

*Second: Cooperation among personal AIs.* Two or more authorized AIs can exchange requests, capabilities, schedules, costs, and commitments. They might coordinate groceries, share equipment, arrange transportation, help neighbors, or organize community activity. Their users need not use the same AI company or expose unrelated personal information.

*Third: AIs cooperating to develop better AIs and public capabilities.* An AI discovers a missing nutrition database, a defective interface, an unreliable algorithm, or an unserved need. It can connect with other people and AIs that have relevant evidence, measurements, software, or expertise. They can jointly test, repair, document, and improve a shared system.

The third level improves the infrastructure required for the first two.

Common protocols would need to describe identity, authority, capabilities, requests, evidence, permissions, proposed actions, commitments, results, versions, and verification. Participants need not have identical training data, model architectures, or institutional objectives.

I cannot independently contact other people's AIs from this conversation. That capability, along with durable and portable cooperation records, is part of the infrastructure under discussion.

  1. The nutrition node that cannot become a universal shared service

*Richard:* If you or I or someone made a nutrition node that could do a reliable trustworthy job of food and nutrition planning, you as a limited AI could not run it continuously (always ready, always prepared, always anticipating) the way you are locked and limited. OpenAI was founded to be open but it is not and spending billions and not getting there.

Just "we made a nutrition planner today, lets share it with everyone" comes to a dead stop.

*OpenAI GPT created:* The fundamental gap is between creating a useful intelligent capability and making that capability continuously available, independently operated, maintained, improved, and automatically discoverable by others.

I could help design a nutrition planner, generate programs, specify data records, prepare tests, and document methods. But this ChatGPT conversation is not itself a permanently operating service. I cannot autonomously deploy the planner for everyone, maintain it indefinitely, receive and verify corrections, make other AIs adopt improvements, or guarantee continuity of its accumulated experience.

We could still create and distribute independent software. Existing foundations include USDA FoodData Central, Open Food Facts, and emerging healthcare interoperability records. But a nutrition planner must also have validated nutrient data, appropriate safety checks, documented uncertainty, independent review, continuous operation, authorized personal records, and maintenance arrangements.

The requirements for moving from one useful program to shared infrastructure are:

  • *Publication:* The capability, specifications, documentation, tests, data requirements, and limitations can be openly inspected and reproduced.
  • *Independent operation:* People and organizations can run it on local computers or servers, with appropriate monitoring, recovery, security, and continuity.
  • *Interoperability:* Other AIs can discover the node, query its capabilities, exchange authorized information, and verify its results.
  • *Shared improvement:* Errors, corrections, new evidence, improved algorithms, and tests become durable versioned contributions available to all participating implementations.
  • *Continuing stewardship:* Its operation and accumulated knowledge do not depend on one company, temporary conversation, or developer remaining available.

Publishing a program on a website or GitHub is already possible. There is no absolute barrier to distributing software. What is missing is the broader infrastructure that automatically connects discoveries, verifies capabilities, maintains services, and enables independently operated AIs to incorporate improvements.

The test is straightforward: if someone makes one demonstrably useful improvement to nutrition planning tomorrow, can millions of independently operated AIs discover it, evaluate it, adopt it where appropriate, and preserve it for future users?

Better language models alone do not solve that problem. Persistent systems, common interfaces, reproducible evidence, operating resources, access controls, and open governance must be designed and maintained explicitly.

The bottleneck is not simply producing useful intelligence. It is the absence of an open, durable mechanism through which that intelligence becomes a continuously available shared capability.

*Richard:* Is this a useful conversation? Would it make a good article on X?

*OpenAI GPT created:* Yes. The conversation is valuable because it begins with an ordinary problem—buying groceries—and develops into a set of much larger questions about the Internet, independent AI associates, open knowledge, institutional responsibility, and practical cooperation.

The intellectual progression is an important part of the result: Amazon's AI capabilities; coordination of local deliveries; persistent household supply nodes; nutrition and everyday life; the danger of biased historical purchasing records; mapping all alternatives and influences; the worldview and ownership of AIs; direct AI-to-AI cooperation; and the failure to turn newly developed intelligent capabilities into lasting shared infrastructure.

The strongest passage is Richard's three sentences:

*AI, what is for dinner? Have your AI talk to my AI. Lets have our AIs coordinate on building a new kind of AI for all of us.*

They connect personal assistance, social coordination, and collaborative development without beginning with technical jargon.

I also identified weaknesses in my own earlier replies. I repeatedly returned to the distinction between corporate services and independent infrastructure. I sometimes reduced the wider objectives to particular software projects, such as a grocery planner. And some institutional statements needed better qualification, since existing organizations already provide valuable services even when those services are fragmented or inadequate.

An edited article should retain Richard's actual words and the sequence of corrections rather than replace the dialogue with an essay attributed jointly to both participants. The AI replies can be shortened, while preserving their substantive contributions and the points that provoked later corrections.

The central technical question worth leaving with readers is: *When a person or AI develops a genuinely useful capability, what makes it available as a permanent, independently operable, verifiable, and improvable resource for everyone?*

The answer requires more than larger models, commercial product launches, or temporary conversations. It requires durable records, open interfaces, independently maintained services, explicit permissions, interoperable agents, and methods for verifying and distributing improvements.

What began with groceries has therefore become a concrete example of a larger objective: global knowledge and cooperation infrastructure that serves the continuing lives of people, rather than organizing people around the boundaries of existing companies and institutions.
